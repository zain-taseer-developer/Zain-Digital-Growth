    Website performance

    Simple ways to make your website feel faster

    A slow page can frustrate visitors, but the cause is not always obvious. Start by measuring and checking the parts that commonly add unnecessary weight.

    Before changing your hosting or rebuilding a website, find out where the delay occurs. Try a few important pages on both a phone and a desktop connection. Note whether the page takes a long time to appear, shifts while loading or becomes difficult to use. A performance report can help identify likely causes, though real-device checks still matter.

    Review large images first

    Photos are often among the largest files on a page. Use appropriately sized images instead of uploading a full-resolution original when a smaller version will look just as good. Choose a modern, well-supported format where your site allows it, and avoid using large images as decoration if they do not help the visitor.

    Be selective with extra scripts

    Chat widgets, trackers, embedded feeds and other third-party tools can add requests and code. Check which tools are still useful and remove anything that is no longer needed. Each external service is also something to keep updated and consider in your privacy disclosures.

    Test the pages that matter most

    Prioritize the homepage, service pages and key contact or booking steps. A fast homepage does not compensate for a slow booking form. Check that improvements do not break menus, forms, images or other essential features on smaller screens.

    A sensible performance routine

    • Measure a few important pages before making changes.
    • Resize and compress images while keeping them clear.
    • Review plug-ins, scripts and embedded content for ongoing value.
    • Retest the same pages and devices after each meaningful change.
    • Keep accessibility and reliability in view alongside speed.

    Website performance is an ongoing part of maintenance rather than a one-time score. Make measured changes, keep the visitor experience intact and focus on improvements people can actually feel.
