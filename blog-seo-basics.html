    SEO basics

    SEO basics for a small business website

    You do not need a complicated campaign to make a useful start. Focus first on helping people and search engines understand what your business offers.

    Search engine optimization can feel like a long list of technical tasks. For a small business, the most dependable first steps are usually simpler: create pages that answer real questions, make those pages easy to navigate, and keep your business information accurate.

    Give each page one clear job

    A service page should explain one service, who it is for and what a customer can expect. A location page should help local visitors understand where and how you work. When several pages repeat the same broad message, it becomes harder for readers to find the detail they need.

    Use a descriptive page title and a plain-language heading. Write the page for a person first: explain your process, answer likely questions and include a clear next step. Search terms fit naturally when they describe what customers already call the service.

    Make the site easy to explore

    Simple navigation helps visitors move between related services and contact details. Link pages together where it genuinely helps a reader, and check that important pages work well on a phone. A clean structure is more useful than adding lots of pages just to target slightly different phrases.

    Check the basics regularly

    • Make sure every important page has a useful title and one clear main heading.
    • Use descriptive links instead of labels such as “click here”.
    • Keep contact details and service information current.
    • Review search performance over time rather than judging by a single day.

    Be patient and keep improving

    SEO is not an instant switch. Search engines need time to discover changes, and results depend on your market, site and competitors. Start with the pages customers rely on most, improve them based on real questions, and make changes you can maintain.

    The best first SEO task is often the one that makes a page genuinely clearer. Once the fundamentals are in place, you can use search data to decide what deserves attention next.
